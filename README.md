Customer Behavior & Churn Analysis — E-Commerce Dashboard
Overview
This project analyzes transactional data from an online retailer to identify the behaviors and patterns most associated with customer churn. The goal is to give decision-makers clear, actionable insights into which customer segments are at risk, which product categories drive the most value, and where targeted retention strategies would have the greatest impact.

Business Questions
This project was built around five core questions:

How do purchase frequency and discount usage correlate with customer retention?
Which customer segments are most likely to churn?
Which product categories are most affected by churn?
What behaviors signal a high risk of churn?
What strategies can the company use to retain high-value customers?


Dashboard
The dashboard is organized across two pages, each targeting a different audience and purpose.
Performance Overview
This page is designed for executives who need a fast read on overall business health. It includes KPI cards showing Total Sales, Total Profit, and Discounts at a glance, a combo chart comparing Sales vs. Profit by Product Category, and a time series chart with a sales forecast showing continued growth.
Key Finding: The Fashion category significantly outpaces all others in both sales and profit. Electronics show the lowest profitability and represent the clearest area for strategic review.
Strategic Opportunities
This page supports deeper analysis and scenario planning. It segments products into three clusters based on sales volume and profitability — high-performing products that warrant sustained marketing investment, mid-tier products with room for targeted improvement, and low-performing products that need optimization or reconsideration. It also includes an interactive what-if simulation showing how profit margins shift across categories under different business scenarios, and a drill-down view breaking down sales by individual product.

Key Findings

Fashion is the strongest category across all metrics and should receive continued marketing investment.
Electronics has a weak profit margin relative to its sales volume, signaling a need to revisit pricing and supplier contracts.
Discount usage is a significant lever — moderate-performing segments benefit from targeted promotional activity without eroding margins.
Customer clusters reveal a clear divide between high-value loyal buyers and at-risk segments that are under-engaged or over-discounted.
